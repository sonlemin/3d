- **Core fantasy: the clever survivor.** "Hold a bridge against the dead with nothing but your wits, your ears and the bridge itself."
  - Chosen over three alternatives:
    - *The one who remembers*: the defense risked becoming a chore between story moments.
    - *The listener in the dark*: it would rest the whole game on the unproven sound radar.
    - *Both, fused*: two fantasies, and the player gets neither fully.
  - Why: it's what the player does every minute, it matches the brainstorm's audience order (tense strategy first), and it fits CJ2 (the rules work without the lore).
  - Consequence: remembering is the reward and must not replace the defense. Core Fundamentals will settle how names stay earned (H2 against P8, R4 and Y3).
- **What players walk away with: pride, then peace.** Chosen over peace alone, haunted, and mastery. It follows the arc of the cries (E4).
- **Vision drafted** in `brief.md` with the recommended pitch and vision statement, plus a spoiler-free public pitch. Waiting for Sonlm's markup.
- **Vision approved as drafted.**
  - Both pitches stay: the full pitch, which reveals the truth in Chapter 3 (M14), is for the GDD; the spoiler-free pitch is for the store page and other public text.
  - Vision statement: "make players feel clever for barely surviving the dead, then make them care who the dead were." Rejected: "every victory has a name" (leans toward the story side) and "holding on / letting go" (the most ambitious).
- **Primary audience: day/night strategy players** (*Thronefall*, *Dome Keeper*, *Kingdom Two Crowns*).
  - Rejected:
    - Up-close trap-defense players: they expect to fight, and *Orcs Must Die! Deathtrap* sits at 73%.
    - Atmospheric horror players: a small group (*Perception* has 550 reviews).
    - Story players: they contradict the survival-first core.
  - Design commitment that follows: a readable board when planning (the overhead view by day) and fair information at night (every threat can be heard, or visually cued, in time to react).
- **Secondary audiences:** story players second, atmosphere and horror players third (the brainstorm's order, kept). Streamers and free-game hunters are how word spreads, not target audiences.
- **Session length:** one day-and-night cycle takes 20–30 minutes, and dawn is the save point. Campaign length isn't decided; for reference, 12–15 nights would be about 4–7 hours.
- **Age and experience:** 16+, has played some strategy games; tower defense experience isn't required.
- **Target Players & Market drafted** in `brief.md` from these picks and the market research. Waiting for Sonlm's markup.

- **Four pillars:**

  - *Hold it by ear*
  - *The bridge is everything*
  - *Every victory has a name*
  - Rejected: three pillars (nothing would protect the second half of the vision statement) and two pillars (being unarmed and the names become negotiable).
- **Night rhythm: safe days first, then the tide.**
  - Chapter 1 has safe days (R1, with dusk and dawn as the clock). The Swell Season (Chapter 2) brings the tide (R2 + R4: the dead come on their own schedule, even while you're below). The finale is one unbroken night (R3).
  - This replaces the brainstorm's default D1, which had the tide from the start (it became the default when Sonlm answered "all").
  - Rejected: D1 as it stands (no safe phase, less like *Thronefall*) and D2 throughout. Modes get revisited in Scope & MVP.
- **Names: earned below, one soul each.**
  - Names are found mostly in the depths (P8), going down costs rigging time, unused names fade (Y3), and each name frees only its own soul. Calling one takes a single action (RB9).
  - Rejected: making the caller exposed at the bell rope, and names only at dawn (which loses the moments where a name stops one of the dead mid-wave).

  - Why: it speaks to the primary audience, shows "barely surviving" in one shot, and doesn't depend on the ear test, which hasn't been run yet.
