Four directions for the screens of the one-night game

Each direction draws the same four screens with the same content, so the only thing that changes is the look: planning (the span from above with the tool tray), dawn (the ledger beside the bridge), the night caption, and the title.

The plan shown is one worked example: 8 planks and 5 ropes stripped, a trapdoor on section 2 and a swing-beam on section 4 paid for, leaving 4 planks and 1 rope in hand. The number under each section is its strength.

Layout is not decided here. The tray sits in a different place in each direction to show the range; the name and epitaph in the ledger are stand-ins until the six real ones are written.

A

Chalk on slate

The ally's tally board, extended to every screen.

All screen text is chalk handwriting on dark slate, the same hand that chalks the tolls at the town pier. Lantern amber marks the one thing chosen. Loose, warm and human; the least formal of the four.

B

Ledger paper

The warden's own book: aged paper, iron-gall ink, a red rubric.

Tray, journal and ledger are pages laid beside a dark world, set in a seventeenth-century book face. Red ink marks the choice. The brightest surfaces of the four, so it reads strongest at dawn and in planning.

C

Lantern light

No panels at all: words lit, or left in the dark.

Text sits straight on black in a light, widely spaced serif. What is chosen glows warm as if a lantern were held to it; everything else is the colourless grey of the dead. The quietest and sparsest.

D

Engraved plate

The woodcut's own vocabulary: black, white, and hatching.

Heavy rules, hatched fills and a broken-letter display face, as if every screen were cut from the same block as the night. A choice is shown by inverting it. The loudest and most graphic.
